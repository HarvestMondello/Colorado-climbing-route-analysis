## Colorado Speed Climbing

Colorado has a long tradition of climbers moving fast and light on routes, often measuring timed efforts as complete round trips from trailhead to trailhead.

<br>

### FKT (Fastest Known Time): 

The fastest documented and verifiable time for completing a defined route or course under an established set of rules. Unlike a formal race record, an FKT may be attempted independently and at any time.

<br>

### Colorado Speed Climbing

Speed-climbing techniques such as simul-climbing (also called a running belay) were developed and refined locally by climbers including Bill Wright and Hans Florine. Colorado also has a strong endurance tradition represented by runners such as Buz Burrell, co-founder and former co-owner of Fastest Known Time (FKT.com), which focuses primarily on running endurance FKTs and generally excludes technical rock-climbing FKTs.

These traditions of fast climbing, running, and efficient mountain travel helped shape FKT-style efforts in Colorado and influenced speed climbing beyond the state, including in Yosemite.

Bill Wright also founded the Satan’s Minions Scrambling Club, which became an important part of Boulder’s speed-climbing and scrambling culture. The club attracted already accomplished climbers and mountain runners, while also providing an environment in which others developed into prominent speed climbers through regular scrambling, time trials, route familiarity, and competition. Members hold many of the FKTs on this website. 

<br>

### Safety Through Speed and Efficiency

On long routes, especially in alpine environments, efficient rope and gear management, economical movement, and effective team communication can improve safety.

Moving efficiently can reduce exposure to objective hazards such as thunderstorms, rain, and rockfall, as well as subjective risks such as poor rope management, route delays, or being caught on the route after dark.

The goal is not simply to move fast, but to eliminate unnecessary delays while maintaining safe climbing practices.

Speed climbing can also increase risk: faster movement, simul-climbing, reduced protection, and hurried transitions can increase the consequences of mistakes or falls. Climbers should choose tactics appropriate to their experience, the route, and current conditions.

Some FKTs are attempted solo, including occasional free-solo ascents. Free soloing removes the protection of a rope, making a fall potentially fatal; it is not a requirement of speed climbing, and most FKTs can be attempted using roped climbing techniques, with or without the added safety of a climbing partner. 

<br>

## Colorado Speed Climbing vs. Yosemite Speed Climbing

Speed climbing in Colorado often differs from the traditional Yosemite model.

In Yosemite, many well-known speed records, such as the Nose on El Capitan, measure the technical climb itself from the base of the route to the top.

In Colorado, approaches and descents are often part of the challenge. This is especially true in Rocky Mountain National Park and on the Fountain Formation of Eldorado Canyon and the Flatirons, where routes may involve strenuous approaches, scrambling, technical descents, and complex route finding.

As a result, Colorado FKTs often test more than technical climbing speed. Running, navigation, transitions, descent skills, and overall economy of movement can all affect the final time.

Areas such as Eldorado Canyon and Chautauqua also use established start and finish points near the trailhead, adding another layer of consistency to route records.

<br>

## FKT Styles

### FKT's on this website

Unless otherwise noted, FKTs listed on this website follow an **unsupported**, **trailhead-to-trailhead (TH2TH)** format.

Some routes use other established start and finish points, such as **bridge-to-bridge (B2B)** in Eldorado Canyon or a specific landmark near Chautauqua in the Flatirons.

<br>

### For an Unsupported FKT:

- Climbers carry all climbing equipment used during the attempt.
- Rappelling is permitted when climbers carry the rope and rappel equipment for the entire attempt.
- Pre-installed or fixed rappel lines are not permitted.
- Gear, food, water, or other supplies may not be cached along the route.
- Other climbers or support crews may not provide equipment or supplies during the attempt.
- Other climbers may photograph or film the attempt, provided they do not otherwise assist.

The intent is for the climber or climbing team to complete the entire route, including the approach and descent where applicable, using only the equipment and supplies they carry from the start.

<br>

### Verification and Transparency

FKTs should be supported by clear evidence whenever possible. GPS tracking is the primary modern method of verification and should document the complete effort from the established start to the established finish.

Publicly posting GPS tracks and supporting details on platforms such as Strava, personal blogs, or other accessible websites is encouraged for transparency.

Additional evidence may include photographs, video, witness accounts, trip reports, or other time-stamped records.

Some older FKTs were completed before reliable GPS technology was widely available. In these cases, alternative forms of verification may be accepted on a case-by-case basis depending on the quality and credibility of the available evidence.

The goal is enough documentation for an FKT to be independently reviewed and reasonably verified.

## Colorado FKTs Overview
The most fiercely competative FKT in Colorado in the **Naked Edge** on **Redgarden Wall** in **Eldorado Canyon**. Considered the Nose of Colorado. 

The other FKTs with a long tradition of competition are primarily: 

- The **Casual Route** on the **Diamond** on **Long's Peak** in **Rocky Mountain National Park**.
- The **Yellow Spur** on **Redgarden Wall** in **Eldorado Canyon**
- **DEF** on the **First Flatirion** and **East Face (Standard)** on the **Third Flatiron**.


### RMNP and other alpine

Alpine FKTs typically include long approaches, technical climbing, complex descents, and significant elevation gain. The **Casual Route** on the **Diamond** is one of the most sought-after speed-climbing objectives in the park. Routes are trailhead to trailhead (TH2TH)

Other competative objectives include**Long's Triathlon** via the Casual Route on the **Diamond** on Long's Peak, the **South Face** of **Petit Grepon** and **Kieners Route** on Long's Peak. Also, the winter speed record on **D7** on the **Diamond** on **Long's Peak**. 

[↓ Alpine FKTs](#alpine-fkts)


### Eldo

Eldo is a historic speed-climbing area with established bridge-to-bridge records, technical descents, and a strong tradition of fast multipitch climbing. The most sought-after FKTs include The Naked Edge and Yellow Spur. Routes are bridge to bridge(B2B). Areas in the adjacent Open Space Mountain Parks (OSMP) are included here.

The most competative Eldo FKT's are **Naked Edge** on Redgarden Wall: Tower Two and the **Yellow Spur** on Redgarden Wall: Tower One.

Other competative objectives include **Ruper** on Redgarden Wall and **Bastille Crack** on The Bastille. 

[↓ Eldo FKTs](#eldo-fkts)


### Flatirons

FKTs often combine scrambling, running, route finding, and fast descents. Routes are trailhead to trailhead with a specific starting point for the first three Flatirons. The First Flatiron is the area's most prominent and sought-after FKT followed by the Third. 

The most competative Flatiron FKT's are **DEF** on the First Flatiron** and **East Face** of the Third Flatiron. 

Other competative objectives are linkups of formations such as the **First and Third**, **Trifecta** and **Quinfecta**, this last being the most prestigious of the three. 

[↓ Flatirons FKTs](#flatirons-fkts)
